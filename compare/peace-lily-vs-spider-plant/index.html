Peace Lily vs Spider Plant

The spider plant is the pet-safe one and shrugs off dry air. The peace lily flowers in lower light but is toxic to cats and dogs.

Spider Plant is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Peace Lily is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach. Peace Lily needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Spider Plant wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

What they share: both are rated easy, both take moderate watering and both stay medium.

Key differences between Peace Lily and Spider Plant

Spider Plant is pet-safe, Peace Lily is not

Spider Plant is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Peace Lily is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach.

Peace Lily copes with less light

Peace Lily needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Spider Plant wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

Peace Lily needs damper air

Peace Lily rates 4/5 for humidity - group plants or run a humidifier in winter. Spider Plant rates 2/5, so ordinary room air is fine.

Spider Plant fills out faster

Spider Plant is a fast grower and gives you cuttings or a full pot sooner. Peace Lily is moderate, which means less pruning and repotting but a longer wait.

Different plant types: flowering vs trailing

Peace Lily is a flowering plant and Spider Plant is a trailing plant, so they fill different jobs in a room even when their care overlaps.

They come from different habitats

Peace Lily originates in Central America; Spider Plant in South Africa. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Peace Lily and Spider Plant apart

The peace lily tells you when it is thirsty -- it wilts dramatically, then recovers within hours of a drink -- which makes it oddly good for people who water on instinct. The spider plant never signals; it just keeps producing plantlets on arching stems that you can snip off and root in water.
