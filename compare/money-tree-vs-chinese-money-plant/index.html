Money Tree vs Chinese Money Plant

Two plants sold as 'money plant': the Pachira is a braided floor tree, the Pilea a small coin-leaved tabletop plant that pups. Both are pet-safe.

Money Tree rates 3/5 for humidity - normal household humidity works. Chinese Money Plant rates 2/5, so ordinary room air is fine. Money Tree reaches 90-180+ cm (3-6+ ft) and belongs on the floor. Chinese Money Plant tops out around 15-30 cm (6-12 in), so it suits a shelf or desk.

What they share: both are pet-safe, both are rated easy, both want medium indirect light and both take low watering.

Key differences between Money Tree and Chinese Money Plant

Money Tree needs damper air

Money Tree rates 3/5 for humidity - normal household humidity works. Chinese Money Plant rates 2/5, so ordinary room air is fine.

Money Tree is the bigger plant

Money Tree reaches 90-180+ cm (3-6+ ft) and belongs on the floor. Chinese Money Plant tops out around 15-30 cm (6-12 in), so it suits a shelf or desk.

Money Tree is the air-purifying one

Money Tree appears on the common indoor air-purifying lists; Chinese Money Plant does not. In a normal room the effect of either is modest, so treat it as a tiebreaker rather than a reason to buy.

They come from different habitats

Money Tree originates in Central America; Chinese Money Plant in China. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Money Tree and Chinese Money Plant apart

Two different plants both sold as 'money plant'. The money tree (Pachira) is a braided-trunk floor tree with palmate leaves. The Chinese money plant (Pilea) is a small tabletop plant with round, coin-like leaves on thin stalks that pups constantly. Both are pet-safe.
