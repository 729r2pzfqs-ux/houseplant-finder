Boston Fern vs Maidenhair Fern

The Boston fern forgives a missed watering; the maidenhair dies if the soil dries once, so it is a bathroom or terrarium plant. Both are pet-safe.

Boston Fern is rated medium to care for, Maidenhair Fern hard. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Boston Fern. Maidenhair Fern needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Boston Fern wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

What they share: both are pet-safe and both are happy in the same humidity.

Key differences between Boston Fern and Maidenhair Fern

Boston Fern is the easier plant

Boston Fern is rated medium to care for, Maidenhair Fern hard. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Boston Fern.

Maidenhair Fern copes with less light

Maidenhair Fern needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Boston Fern wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

Maidenhair Fern drinks more

Boston Fern wants high watering (4/5) - keep the soil evenly moist, never fully dry. Maidenhair Fern needs very high watering (5/5): never let the soil dry out, even briefly.

Boston Fern is the bigger plant

Boston Fern reaches 30-90 cm (1-3 ft) and belongs on a side table or plant stand. Maidenhair Fern tops out around 15-30 cm (6-12 in), so it suits a shelf or desk.

They come from different habitats

Boston Fern originates in Tropical regions; Maidenhair Fern in Worldwide tropics. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Boston Fern and Maidenhair Fern apart

The maidenhair is the hard one. It dies if the soil dries out even once, which makes it a terrarium or bathroom plant rather than a shelf plant. A Boston fern forgives a missed watering and just drops a few fronds. Both are pet-safe.
