Nerve Plant vs Polka Dot Plant

The polka dot plant is the easier one and grows faster, but needs pinching. The nerve plant collapses dramatically when dry, then recovers in an hour.

Polka Dot Plant is rated easy to care for, Nerve Plant medium. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Polka Dot Plant. Nerve Plant needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Polka Dot Plant wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

What they share: both are pet-safe and both stay small.

Key differences between Nerve Plant and Polka Dot Plant

Polka Dot Plant is the easier plant

Polka Dot Plant is rated easy to care for, Nerve Plant medium. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Polka Dot Plant.

Nerve Plant copes with less light

Nerve Plant needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Polka Dot Plant wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

Nerve Plant drinks more

Polka Dot Plant wants moderate watering (3/5) - water when the top 2-3 cm is dry, roughly weekly. Nerve Plant needs high watering (4/5): keep the soil evenly moist, never fully dry.

Nerve Plant needs damper air

Nerve Plant rates 5/5 for humidity - needs a humidifier, a terrarium or a steamy bathroom. Polka Dot Plant rates 3/5, so normal household humidity works.

Polka Dot Plant fills out faster

Polka Dot Plant is a fast grower and gives you cuttings or a full pot sooner. Nerve Plant is moderate, which means less pruning and repotting but a longer wait.

They come from different habitats

Nerve Plant originates in South America; Polka Dot Plant in Madagascar. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Nerve Plant and Polka Dot Plant apart

The nerve plant (fittonia) has fine white or pink veining and collapses theatrically when dry -- then recovers within an hour of water. The polka dot plant is speckled rather than veined, grows faster, and gets leggy unless you pinch it back. Both are pet-safe and both want humidity.
