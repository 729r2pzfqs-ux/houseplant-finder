Bird of Paradise vs Majesty Palm

Both are big and light-hungry. The majesty palm is pet-safe but browns in dry air; the bird of paradise needs your brightest window and is toxic to pets.

Key differences between Bird of Paradise and Majesty Palm

Majesty Palm is pet-safe, Bird of Paradise is not

Majesty Palm is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Bird of Paradise is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach.

Bird of Paradise is the easier plant

Bird of Paradise is rated medium to care for, Majesty Palm hard. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Bird of Paradise.

Majesty Palm copes with less light

Majesty Palm needs bright indirect light (4/5) - beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves. Bird of Paradise wants very bright light (5/5), a south or west window with some direct sun.

Majesty Palm drinks more

Bird of Paradise wants moderate watering (3/5) - water when the top 2-3 cm is dry, roughly weekly. Majesty Palm needs high watering (4/5): keep the soil evenly moist, never fully dry.

Majesty Palm needs damper air

Majesty Palm rates 4/5 for humidity - group plants or run a humidifier in winter. Bird of Paradise rates 3/5, so normal household humidity works.

Majesty Palm is the air-purifying one

Majesty Palm appears on the common indoor air-purifying lists; Bird of Paradise does not. In a normal room the effect of either is modest, so treat it as a tiebreaker rather than a reason to buy.

Different plant types: foliage vs palm

Bird of Paradise is a foliage plant and Majesty Palm is a palm plant, so they fill different jobs in a room even when their care overlaps.

They come from different habitats

Bird of Paradise originates in South Africa; Majesty Palm in Madagascar. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Bird of Paradise and Majesty Palm apart

Both are big, thirsty and light-hungry. The bird of paradise has broad paddle leaves that split naturally with age and needs the single brightest spot in the house. The majesty palm is feathery and pet-safe but browns badly in dry indoor air. Neither is a low-light corner plant.
