Aloe Vera vs Zebra Haworthia

Zebra haworthia is the pet-safe, shelf-sized stand-in: white-banded leaves, less light needed. Aloe is bigger, wants more sun and is toxic to pets.

Zebra Haworthia is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Aloe Vera is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach. Zebra Haworthia needs medium indirect light (3/5) - near a bright window, out of direct sun. Aloe Vera wants bright indirect light (4/5), beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves.

What they share: both are rated easy, both take very low watering, both are happy in the same humidity and both stay small.

Key differences between Aloe Vera and Zebra Haworthia

Zebra Haworthia is pet-safe, Aloe Vera is not

Zebra Haworthia is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Aloe Vera is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach.

Zebra Haworthia copes with less light

Zebra Haworthia needs medium indirect light (3/5) - near a bright window, out of direct sun. Aloe Vera wants bright indirect light (4/5), beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves.

Aloe Vera is the air-purifying one

Aloe Vera appears on the common indoor air-purifying lists; Zebra Haworthia does not. In a normal room the effect of either is modest, so treat it as a tiebreaker rather than a reason to buy.

They come from different habitats

Aloe Vera originates in Arabian Peninsula; Zebra Haworthia in South Africa. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Aloe Vera and Zebra Haworthia apart

Zebra haworthia is the small, safe stand-in for aloe. It has raised white bands on dark leaves, stays under 15 cm, tolerates less light, and is non-toxic to pets. Aloe is bigger, needs more sun, and carries gel you can actually use on a burn.
