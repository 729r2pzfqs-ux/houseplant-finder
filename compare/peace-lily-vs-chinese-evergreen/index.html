Peace Lily vs Chinese Evergreen

The Chinese evergreen handles dry air and neglect better; the peace lily flowers and wilts visibly when thirsty. Both are toxic to cats and dogs.

Chinese Evergreen wants low watering (2/5) - water when the top 3-5 cm of soil has dried out. Peace Lily needs moderate watering (3/5): water when the top 2-3 cm is dry, roughly weekly. Peace Lily rates 4/5 for humidity - group plants or run a humidifier in winter. Chinese Evergreen rates 3/5, so normal household humidity works.

What they share: both are toxic to cats and dogs, both are rated easy, both want low to medium light and both stay medium.

Key differences between Peace Lily and Chinese Evergreen

Peace Lily drinks more

Chinese Evergreen wants low watering (2/5) - water when the top 3-5 cm of soil has dried out. Peace Lily needs moderate watering (3/5): water when the top 2-3 cm is dry, roughly weekly.

Peace Lily needs damper air

Peace Lily rates 4/5 for humidity - group plants or run a humidifier in winter. Chinese Evergreen rates 3/5, so normal household humidity works.

Peace Lily fills out faster

Peace Lily is a moderate grower and gives you cuttings or a full pot sooner. Chinese Evergreen is slow, which means less pruning and repotting but a longer wait.

Different plant types: flowering vs foliage

Peace Lily is a flowering plant and Chinese Evergreen is a foliage plant, so they fill different jobs in a room even when their care overlaps.

They come from different habitats

Peace Lily originates in Central America; Chinese Evergreen in Southeast Asia. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Peace Lily and Chinese Evergreen apart

Both are low-light standards. The peace lily flowers and wilts visibly when thirsty; the Chinese evergreen does neither but handles dry air and neglect better and comes in silver, red and pink forms. Both are toxic to pets.
