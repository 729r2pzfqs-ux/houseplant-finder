Bird's Nest Fern vs Staghorn Fern

Choose by mounting, not care: the bird's nest sits in a pot as a rosette, the staghorn hangs on a board and is watered by soaking. Both are pet-safe.

Bird's Nest Fern needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Staghorn Fern wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun. Bird's Nest Fern is a moderate grower and gives you cuttings or a full pot sooner. Staghorn Fern is slow, which means less pruning and repotting but a longer wait.

What they share: both are pet-safe, both are rated medium, both take moderate watering, both are happy in the same humidity and both stay medium.

Bird's Nest Fern if…

  • ✓you would rather not prune and repot often
  • ✓the look is what you are after: Unique antler-shaped fronds. Often mounted on wood or in baskets.

Key differences between Bird's Nest Fern and Staghorn Fern

Bird's Nest Fern copes with less light

Bird's Nest Fern needs low to medium light (2/5) - fine a few metres back from a window. Staghorn Fern wants medium indirect light (3/5), near a bright window, out of direct sun.

Bird's Nest Fern fills out faster

Bird's Nest Fern is a moderate grower and gives you cuttings or a full pot sooner. Staghorn Fern is slow, which means less pruning and repotting but a longer wait.

They come from different habitats

Bird's Nest Fern originates in Southeast Asia; Staghorn Fern in Australia. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Bird's Nest Fern and Staghorn Fern apart

The bird's nest grows in a pot as a rosette of wavy, undivided fronds. The staghorn is an epiphyte usually mounted on a board, watered by soaking, and hung on a wall. Choose by how you want to mount it, not by care.
