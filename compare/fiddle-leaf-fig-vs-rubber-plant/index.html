Fiddle Leaf Fig vs Rubber Plant

Take the rubber plant: rated easy, less light, less water, far more neglect tolerated. The fiddle leaf fig is rated hard and drops leaves over anything.

Rubber Plant is rated easy to care for, Fiddle Leaf Fig hard. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Rubber Plant. Rubber Plant needs bright indirect light (4/5) - beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves. Fiddle Leaf Fig wants very bright light (5/5), a south or west window with some direct sun.

What they share: both are toxic to cats and dogs and both stay large.

Key differences between Fiddle Leaf Fig and Rubber Plant

Rubber Plant is the easier plant

Rubber Plant is rated easy to care for, Fiddle Leaf Fig hard. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Rubber Plant.

Rubber Plant copes with less light

Rubber Plant needs bright indirect light (4/5) - beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves. Fiddle Leaf Fig wants very bright light (5/5), a south or west window with some direct sun.

Fiddle Leaf Fig drinks more

Rubber Plant wants low watering (2/5) - water when the top 3-5 cm of soil has dried out. Fiddle Leaf Fig needs moderate watering (3/5): water when the top 2-3 cm is dry, roughly weekly.

Fiddle Leaf Fig needs damper air

Fiddle Leaf Fig rates 4/5 for humidity - group plants or run a humidifier in winter. Rubber Plant rates 3/5, so normal household humidity works.

Rubber Plant is the air-purifying one

Rubber Plant appears on the common indoor air-purifying lists; Fiddle Leaf Fig does not. In a normal room the effect of either is modest, so treat it as a tiebreaker rather than a reason to buy.

They come from different habitats

Fiddle Leaf Fig originates in West Africa; Rubber Plant in Southeast Asia. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Fiddle Leaf Fig and Rubber Plant apart

If you want a big-leaved indoor tree and you do not want drama, buy the rubber plant. It takes less light, less water and far more neglect than a fiddle leaf fig, which drops leaves whenever anything changes. The fiddle's leaves are bigger and more sculptural -- that is what you are paying for.
