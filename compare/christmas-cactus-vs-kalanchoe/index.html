Christmas Cactus vs Kalanchoe

The Christmas cactus is pet-safe and wants indirect light and regular water. The kalanchoe needs full sun and almost none, and is toxic to cats and dogs.

Christmas Cactus is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Kalanchoe is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach. Christmas Cactus needs medium indirect light (3/5) - near a bright window, out of direct sun. Kalanchoe wants bright indirect light (4/5), beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves.

What they share: both are rated easy.

Key differences between Christmas Cactus and Kalanchoe

Christmas Cactus is pet-safe, Kalanchoe is not

Christmas Cactus is non-toxic to cats and dogs. Kalanchoe is toxic to cats and dogs if chewed, so it needs a shelf pets cannot reach.

Christmas Cactus copes with less light

Christmas Cactus needs medium indirect light (3/5) - near a bright window, out of direct sun. Kalanchoe wants bright indirect light (4/5), beside a bright window, no midday sun on the leaves.

Christmas Cactus drinks more

Kalanchoe wants low watering (2/5) - water when the top 3-5 cm of soil has dried out. Christmas Cactus needs moderate watering (3/5): water when the top 2-3 cm is dry, roughly weekly.

Christmas Cactus needs damper air

Christmas Cactus rates 3/5 for humidity - normal household humidity works. Kalanchoe rates 1/5, so ordinary dry room air is fine.

Christmas Cactus is the bigger plant

Christmas Cactus reaches 30-90 cm (1-3 ft) and belongs on a side table or plant stand. Kalanchoe tops out around 15-30 cm (6-12 in), so it suits a shelf or desk.

Different plant types: cactus vs flowering

Christmas Cactus is a cactus plant and Kalanchoe is a flowering plant, so they fill different jobs in a room even when their care overlaps.

They come from different habitats

Christmas Cactus originates in Brazil; Kalanchoe in Madagascar. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Christmas Cactus and Kalanchoe apart

The Christmas cactus is a jungle cactus -- it wants indirect light, regular water, and a cool dark autumn to set buds, and it is safe for pets. The kalanchoe is a true succulent that needs direct sun, very little water, and is toxic to cats and dogs.
