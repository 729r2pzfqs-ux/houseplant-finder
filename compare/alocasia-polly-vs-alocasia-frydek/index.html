Alocasia Polly vs Alocasia Frydek

Frydek is the slightly more forgiving one - velvet leaves, rated medium where Polly is hard. Both go dormant and drop everything if cold or dry.

Key differences between Alocasia Polly and Alocasia Frydek

Alocasia Frydek is the easier plant

Alocasia Frydek is rated medium to care for, Alocasia Polly hard. If this is an early houseplant or the spot is hard to get right, start with the Alocasia Frydek.

Alocasia Polly needs damper air

Alocasia Polly rates 5/5 for humidity - needs a humidifier, a terrarium or a steamy bathroom. Alocasia Frydek rates 4/5, so group plants or run a humidifier in winter.

Alocasia Frydek is the air-purifying one

Alocasia Frydek appears on the common indoor air-purifying lists; Alocasia Polly does not. In a normal room the effect of either is modest, so treat it as a tiebreaker rather than a reason to buy.

They come from different habitats

Alocasia Polly originates in Southeast Asia; Alocasia Frydek in Philippines. Matching the original habitat is the quickest way to guess what each one wants indoors.

How to tell Alocasia Polly and Alocasia Frydek apart

Frydek has velvet, matte-green leaves with bright white veins and is the softer-looking of the two; Polly's are glossy, stiff and more sharply lobed. Frydek is marginally more forgiving, but both go dormant and drop everything if they get cold or dry.
